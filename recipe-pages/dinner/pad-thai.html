About This Recipe

This pad thai recipe ive tried to keep somewhat traditonal but also a little easier to cook and find ingredients. This rendition still using mostly simular ingredients but does swap out tamarind paste which is usually used for rice wine vinegar. This pad thai is a well rounded dish with sweet, salty and sour tastes that balence each other and harmonise together with a savoury umami depth. It inpresses as well as beng a quick cook, this is a recipe id bring out for a week night dinner or when impressing guests.

Ingredients

  • Rice noodles
  • Prawns or tofu
  • Onion or shallot
  • Garlic
  • Ginger
  • Spring onion
  • Bean sprouts
  • Lime
  • Soy sauce
  • Fish sauce
  • Rice wine vinegar
  • Brown sugar
  • Salt & Pepper
  • Chilli flakes
  • Oil
  • (Optional)Crushed peanuts

How To Make

  1. 1. First of all you want to prepare your tofu or prawns, cut or peel to size and cook in wok/ pan on a medium high heat with oil.
  2. 2. Once cooked set aside but keep pan hot. Dice your onion and put into pan. Then dice your garlic, ginger and spring onion root ends and put into pan. Season with salt and add chilli flakes.
  3. 3. Next boil water in your kettel and pour over your noodles in a container, let them sit in boiled water for 6-8 minutes or until cooked.
  4. 4. Next you want to make your sauce, for this in a bowl mix rice wine vinegar, soy sauce, fish sauce, lime juice and sugar. Mix until sugar has disolved and is fully mixed.
  5. 5. Add bean sprouts to the pan. Once noodles are cooked, drain and add to your pan, stir fry until noodles start to crisp on edges then add your sauce. Cook for 30 seconds until the sauce starts caremelising, then add your protein back in.
  6. 6. Add a squeeze of lime into the pan then plate up.
  7. 7. Finish with a garnish of a lime wedge and the tops of your spring onion as well as crushed peanuts if you want.

A few tips to perfect the recipe


  • If cooking with prawns, slicing down the back to butterfly it can help cook quicker.

  • Taste your dish once its nearly done and adjust the taste. Rice wine vinegar and lime adds a sour taste. Fish sauce and soy sauce adds a savoury and umami depth with saltiness. And the sugar adds a sweetness to it that balances the sauce.

  • You can change the ratios of soy and fish sauce depending on if you like fish sauce. If not add more soy and less fish. Fish sauce can be an acquired taste, however cooking will mellow this.
